Enabling transparent crowdfunding across Africa.

Fundraising in Africa is full of challenges — people in need often struggle to connect with willing donors, feel ashamed to ask for help, and donors fear being misled. ELPX was built to solve this with a transparent, inclusive, and accessible crowdfunding platform.

01

The Problem

We summarized the core barriers as CAMTA: Connection, Advocacy, Motivation, Trust, Ability.

Difficulty connecting donors and fundraisers, shame or fear in asking for help, lack of incentive for donors to give, trust concerns over misuse of funds, and financial constraints on donors all compounded to keep fundraising inaccessible for the people who needed it most.

  • Enable seamless campaign creation for individuals, NGOs, and communities
  • Build trust through verification and transparent fund tracking
  • Motivate giving through storytelling and social sharing
  • Ensure mobile accessibility for wide adoption
  • Support scalable, role-based access for admins and super admins
02

Research

We spoke mostly with fundraisers and people involved in running or supporting fundraising campaigns, trying to understand where the biggest challenges were around getting campaigns seen, building trust, and getting people to actually contribute.

Those conversations surfaced recurring pain points around visibility, credibility, and motivation to give, the raw material that later became the CAMTA framework.

  • Interviews with fundraisers and campaign organizers/supporters
  • Focus areas: campaign visibility, donor trust, contribution motivation
03

Synthesis

The workshop was a synthesis and co-creation exercise. We brought the interview findings together, looked for recurring patterns and pain points, and worked through them as a team.

CAMTA (Connection, Advocacy, Motivation, Trust, Ability) came out of that process, our way of turning what we'd learned from research into a framework that could guide product and design decisions.

  • Team workshop synthesizing interview findings into recurring patterns
  • Connection: difficulty linking donors and fundraisers
  • Advocacy & Motivation: shame in asking, low incentive to give
  • Trust: concerns over fund misuse
  • Ability: financial constraints on donors

My Role

As the sole Product Designer on ELPX, I focused on user flows, interfaces, and scalable design systems to ensure accessibility, trust, and usability across the platform.

That included designing onboarding and campaign creation flows for individuals, NGOs, and communities, building dashboards for managing campaigns and donations, and creating one consistent design system across every user role — donor, fundraiser, and admin.

Collaboration & Handoff

Collaborated iteratively with a small engineering team rather than handing off designs at the end, using Figma for the component and design-system handoff and clarifying interactions and edge cases directly with engineers as the system was implemented across every user role, from donor to admin.

05

Business Impact

  • Designed user flows for campaign creation, donation, and admin management to enable a scalable platform
  • Established a design system for consistent interfaces across creators, donors, and admins
  • Aligned design direction with ELPX's mission of trust, transparency, and inclusivity
  • Delivered prototypes and visuals positioning ELPX as a credible African-first crowdfunding solution ready for market entry

Reflection

ELPX sharpened how I think about designing for trust as a first-class requirement, not an afterthought — the CAMTA framework meant every screen had to do more than look clean; it had to actively lower the emotional and practical barriers to asking for or giving help.

If I were starting over, I'd push harder to test the trust-building elements — verification badges, fund tracking — specifically with donors who are naturally skeptical, since that's a different bar than general usability testing.
